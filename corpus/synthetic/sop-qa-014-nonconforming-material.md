# SOP-QA-014 — Nonconforming Material Disposition Procedure

> **SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT.** This is a fictional standard operating procedure authored for this portfolio project, illustrating typical QMS nonconformance-disposition structure. It does not describe a real facility's actual procedure and does not reproduce any actual ISO or other standard's text.

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines the requirements for identifying, segregating, evaluating, and dispositioning material found to be nonconforming to specification at any point in the manufacturing process — incoming inspection, in-process production, or final inspection.

## 2. Scope

Applies to all raw material, work-in-process, and finished goods identified as nonconforming at this facility, regardless of the stage of production at which nonconformance is detected.

## 3. Definitions

- **Nonconforming material**: material that does not meet one or more specified requirements (dimensional, functional, cosmetic, or documentation-related).
- **Disposition**: the documented decision on what happens to nonconforming material — Use-As-Is, Rework, Repair, Return-to-Supplier, or Scrap.
- **Material Review Board (MRB)**: the cross-functional group (Quality, Engineering, and Production representatives at minimum) authorized to approve dispositions other than Scrap for material affecting form, fit, or function.

## 4. Procedure

### 4.1 Identification and segregation

1. Any employee who identifies suspected nonconforming material shall immediately stop using it, affix a "Nonconforming — Hold" tag stating the part number, quantity, lot/serial number, and a brief description of the nonconformance, and physically move the material to the designated hold area (or, for material too large to move, cordon it off and mark it clearly in place).
2. The employee shall notify their supervisor and the quality department within the same shift. For nonconformances discovered in a lot that has already partially shipped or been used in downstream assembly, notification shall be immediate regardless of shift end, given the potential need for containment or recall action.
3. A nonconformance report (NCR) shall be opened in the quality system within one business day of identification, documenting the part number, quantity affected, nonconformance description, where/how it was discovered, and the lot/serial traceability.

### 4.2 Evaluation

4. The quality engineer shall determine whether the nonconformance is isolated to the identified material or may extend to other lots, other shifts' production, or other locations using the same process/material/supplier — and shall expand the hold/quarantine accordingly before completing the evaluation.
5. For nonconformances that do not affect form, fit, or function (e.g., minor cosmetic issues on a non-customer-visible surface, within an established use-as-is precedent), the quality engineer may disposition the material directly.
6. For all other nonconformances, the NCR is routed to the Material Review Board for disposition. The MRB shall document its rationale for the chosen disposition on the NCR, including reference to the specific requirement not met and any test/analysis supporting the disposition decision.

### 4.3 Disposition options

- **Use-As-Is**: the nonconformance does not affect form, fit, function, or the customer's specified requirements, and use of the material as-is is documented and approved by the MRB (or quality engineer per Section 4.2, Step 5).
- **Rework**: the material is returned to a standard, previously-qualified process step to bring it into conformance (e.g., re-machining to print dimensions). Reworked material is re-inspected against the original acceptance criteria before release.
- **Repair**: the material is processed via a non-standard method to make it usable, though it may not fully conform to the original specification (e.g., a cosmetic touch-up). Repair dispositions require MRB approval and, where the affected characteristic is customer-specified, customer notification/approval per the applicable contract terms.
- **Return-to-Supplier**: for incoming-inspection nonconformances attributable to the supplier, material is returned under a supplier corrective action request per SOP-QA-008.
- **Scrap**: the material is permanently marked as unusable (e.g., stamped, defaced, or physically destroyed per the facility's scrap procedure) and removed from usable inventory. Scrap disposition does not require MRB approval but does require quality engineer sign-off and an inventory adjustment.

### 4.4 Closure

7. Once disposition is complete (rework/repair verified conforming, use-as-is documented, return-to-supplier shipped, or scrap confirmed), the NCR is closed by the quality engineer with the final disposition, quantity by disposition category (if a lot was split across dispositions), and any corrective action reference.
8. Where the root cause investigation identifies a systemic issue (e.g., a worn tool, an out-of-calibration gauge, an incorrect work instruction), a corrective action request shall be opened separately per the facility's corrective action procedure — closing the NCR does not close the underlying corrective action.

## 5. Records

NCRs, MRB disposition records, and associated corrective action references shall be retained for a minimum of 3 years or per the applicable customer contract's record retention requirement, whichever is longer, and shall be traceable to the affected lot/serial numbers for recall purposes if needed.
