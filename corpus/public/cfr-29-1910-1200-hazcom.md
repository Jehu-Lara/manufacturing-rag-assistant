# 29 CFR 1910.1200 — Hazard Communication

> U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, Title 29 (Labor), Part 1910, Subpart Z (Toxic and Hazardous Substances), § 1910.1200. Public domain (U.S. government work, 17 U.S.C. §105). This excerpt covers the core regulatory text, paragraphs (a) through (j). The section's six mandatory technical appendices — Appendix A (Health Hazard Criteria), Appendix B (Physical Hazard Criteria), Appendix C (Allocation of Label Elements), Appendix D (Safety Data Sheets), Appendix E (Definition of Trade Secret), and Appendix F (Guidance for Hazard Classifications re: Carcinogenicity) — are each individually long, self-contained technical annexes and are omitted here for length; they are part of the same regulation at the source URL above.

## (a) Purpose

(1) The purpose of this section is to ensure that the hazards of all chemicals produced or imported are classified, and that information concerning the classified hazards is transmitted to employers and employees. The requirements of this section are intended to be consistent with the provisions of the United Nations Globally Harmonized System of Classification and Labelling of Chemicals (GHS), Revision 3. The transmittal of information is to be accomplished by means of comprehensive hazard communication programs, which are to include container labeling and other forms of warning, safety data sheets and employee training.

(2) This occupational safety and health standard is intended to address comprehensively the issue of classifying the potential hazards of chemicals, and communicating information concerning hazards and appropriate protective measures to employees, and to preempt any legislative or regulatory enactments of a state, or political subdivision of a state, pertaining to this subject — including, for example, provisions for a written hazard communication program with a hazardous chemical list; labeling of containers in the workplace and containers shipped to other workplaces; preparation and distribution of safety data sheets; and employee training programs. Under section 18 of the Act, no state or political subdivision may adopt or enforce any requirement relating to the issue addressed by this federal standard, except pursuant to a federally-approved state plan.

## (b) Scope and application

(1) This section requires chemical manufacturers or importers to classify the hazards of chemicals which they produce or import, and all employers to provide information to their employees about the hazardous chemicals to which they are exposed, by means of a hazard communication program, labels and other forms of warning, safety data sheets, and information and training. Distributors must transmit the required information to employers. Employers who do not produce or import chemicals need only focus on the parts of this rule dealing with establishing a workplace program and communicating information to their workers.

(2) This section applies to any chemical known to be present in the workplace in such a manner that employees may be exposed under normal conditions of use or in a foreseeable emergency.

(3) **Laboratories** — employers must ensure incoming container labels are not removed/defaced, must maintain and provide ready shift-time access to incoming safety data sheets, and must provide employee information/training under paragraph (h) (except the written-program location/availability item); laboratory employers who ship hazardous chemicals are treated as a chemical manufacturer or distributor and must label outgoing containers under paragraph (f) and provide SDSs under paragraphs (g)(6)-(7).

(4) **Sealed-container-only operations** (e.g. marine cargo handling, warehousing, retail sales) — employers must not remove/deface incoming labels, must maintain/obtain and provide ready shift-time access to SDSs, and must provide information/training under paragraph (h) sufficient to protect employees in the event of a spill or leak.

(5) This section does not require labeling of chemicals already labeled under other federal labeling regimes administered by other agencies, specifically: pesticides under FIFRA (EPA); substances/mixtures under TSCA (EPA); foods, food additives, color additives, drugs, cosmetics, or medical/veterinary devices under the FD&C Act or Virus-Serum-Toxin Act (FDA/USDA); distilled spirits, wine, or malt beverages for nonindustrial use under the Federal Alcohol Administration Act (ATF); consumer products or hazardous substances under the Consumer Product Safety Act or Federal Hazardous Substances Act (CPSC); and agricultural/vegetable seed treated with pesticides under the Federal Seed Act (USDA).

(6) This section does not apply to: hazardous waste under RCRA (EPA); hazardous substances that are the focus of CERCLA remedial/removal action; tobacco or tobacco products; wood or wood products whose only hazard is flammability/combustibility (treated wood, or wood subsequently sawed/cut generating dust, is not exempted); "articles" as defined in paragraph (c); food or alcoholic beverages sold/used/prepared in a retail establishment, and food for personal consumption by employees; drugs in solid final form for direct patient administration, OTC drugs packaged for retail sale, and drugs for employees' personal consumption (e.g. first aid supplies); cosmetics packaged for retail sale or for employees' personal consumption; consumer products/hazardous substances used in the workplace consistent with intended consumer use and exposure; nuisance particulates posing no covered hazard; ionizing and nonionizing radiation; and biological hazards.

## (c) Definitions

Selected key terms as defined in the regulation:

- **Article** — a manufactured item (other than a fluid or particle) formed to a specific shape/design, whose end-use function depends in whole or part on that shape/design, and which under normal use releases no more than very small (trace) quantities of a hazardous chemical and poses no physical or health risk to employees.
- **Chemical** — any substance or mixture of substances. **Substance** — chemical elements and their compounds in the natural state or as produced, including stabilizing additives and process impurities, excluding separable solvents. **Mixture** — a combination or solution of two or more substances that do not react.
- **Chemical manufacturer** — an employer whose workplace produces chemicals for use or distribution. **Importer** — the first business with U.S. employees receiving foreign-produced hazardous chemicals for supply to distributors or employers. **Distributor** — a business (other than manufacturer/importer) supplying hazardous chemicals to other distributors or employers.
- **Classification** — identifying relevant hazard data, reviewing it, and deciding whether/how severely a chemical is classified as hazardous, per Appendix A (health) and Appendix B (physical) criteria.
- **Container** — any bag, barrel, bottle, box, can, cylinder, drum, reaction vessel, or storage tank holding a hazardous chemical (pipes/piping systems and vehicle engines/fuel tanks/operating systems are not containers).
- **Employee** — a worker who may be exposed to hazardous chemicals under normal operating conditions or foreseeable emergencies (excludes workers, e.g. office workers, who encounter such chemicals only in non-routine, isolated instances). **Employer** — a person engaged in a business where chemicals are used, distributed, or produced, including contractors/subcontractors.
- **Exposure / exposed** — an employee subjected in the course of employment to a physical- or health-hazard chemical, including potential exposure, by any route of entry (inhalation, ingestion, skin contact or absorption).
- **Foreseeable emergency** — any potential occurrence (e.g. equipment failure, container rupture, control-equipment failure) that could cause an uncontrolled release of a hazardous chemical into the workplace.
- **Hazard class** — the nature of a physical or health hazard (e.g. flammable solid, carcinogen, oral acute toxicity). **Hazard category** — the division of criteria within a hazard class comparing severity within that class. **Hazard not otherwise classified (HNOC)** — an adverse effect identified through evaluation that does not meet the specified criteria for the hazard classes addressed in this section.
- **Hazardous chemical** — any chemical classified as a physical hazard, a health hazard, a simple asphyxiant, combustible dust, pyrophoric gas, or HNOC. **Health hazard** — a chemical posing acute toxicity, skin corrosion/irritation, serious eye damage/irritation, respiratory or skin sensitization, germ cell mutagenicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, specific target organ toxicity (single or repeated exposure), or aspiration hazard (criteria in Appendix A). **Physical hazard** — explosive, flammable, oxidizer, self-reactive, pyrophoric, self-heating, organic peroxide, corrosive to metal, gas under pressure, or emits flammable gas in contact with water (criteria in Appendix B).
- **Label** — the group of written/printed/graphic hazard information elements affixed to a hazardous chemical's immediate container or outer packaging. **Label elements** — the pictogram, hazard statement, signal word, and precautionary statement specified for each hazard class/category. **Pictogram** — a symbol-plus-graphic-elements composition conveying specific hazard information (eight are designated under this standard). **Signal word** — "Danger" (more severe) or "Warning" (less severe).
- **Safety data sheet (SDS)** — written/printed material about a hazardous chemical prepared per paragraph (g). **Product identifier** — the name/number identifying a hazardous chemical consistently across the hazard communication program list, label, and SDS.
- **Trade secret** — a confidential formula, pattern, process, device, or compilation of information used in a business that gives a competitive advantage (criteria in Appendix E). **Simple asphyxiant** — a substance/mixture that displaces ambient oxygen, causing oxygen deprivation, unconsciousness, and death. **Pyrophoric gas** — a gas that ignites spontaneously in air at ≤130°F (54.4°C).
- **Work area** — a room or defined space in a workplace where hazardous chemicals are produced or used and employees are present. **Workplace** — an establishment, job site, or project at one geographic location containing one or more work areas.

## (d) Hazard classification

(1) Chemical manufacturers and importers shall evaluate chemicals they produce or import to classify them, determining applicable hazard classes and, where appropriate, categories. Employers need not classify chemicals unless they choose not to rely on the manufacturer's/importer's classification.

(2) Classification requires identifying and considering the full range of available scientific literature and other evidence — there is no requirement to test the chemical. Appendix A governs health hazard classification; Appendix B governs physical hazard classification.

(3) **Mixtures** — Appendices A and B procedures govern mixture classification and coverage determinations. Manufacturers/importers of mixtures may rely on current SDSs of individual ingredients, except where they know (or with reasonable diligence should know) that an ingredient SDS misstates or omits required information.

## (e) Written hazard communication program

(1) Employers shall develop, implement, and maintain at each workplace a written hazard communication program describing how the requirements of paragraphs (f), (g), and (h) (labels/warnings, SDSs, employee information/training) will be met, including: (i) a list of hazardous chemicals known to be present, referenced by product identifier to the appropriate SDS (workplace-wide or per work area); and (ii) methods for informing employees of non-routine-task hazards (e.g. cleaning reactor vessels) and hazards of chemicals in unlabeled pipes.

(2) **Multi-employer workplaces** — an employer whose hazardous chemicals may expose other employers' employees on-site (e.g. a construction contractor's workers) must additionally ensure the program covers: (i) methods for giving other employers on-site access to relevant SDSs; (ii) methods for informing other employers of precautionary measures for normal operations and foreseeable emergencies; and (iii) methods for informing other employers of the workplace's labeling system.

(3) An employer may rely on an existing hazard communication program if it meets this paragraph's criteria.

(4) The written program shall be made available, upon request, to employees, their designated representatives, the Assistant Secretary, and the Director, per 29 CFR 1910.20(e).

(5) Where employees travel between workplaces during a shift, the written program may be kept at the primary workplace facility.

## (f) Labels and other forms of warning

(1) **Labels on shipped containers** — the chemical manufacturer, importer, or distributor shall ensure each hazardous-chemical container leaving the workplace is labeled, tagged, or marked (HNOC need not be addressed on the container) with: (i) product identifier; (ii) signal word; (iii) hazard statement(s); (iv) pictogram(s); (v) precautionary statement(s); and (vi) name, address, and telephone number of the manufacturer, importer, or other responsible party.

(2)-(3) This label information must follow Appendix C for each hazard class/category, be prominently displayed, in English (other languages may be added), with the signal word, hazard statement(s), and pictogram(s) located together on the label.

(4) **Solid materials** (e.g. steel beams, metal castings, solid wood, plastic items not exempted as articles, whole-grain shipments) — the required label may be transmitted at initial shipment only (with the shipment itself or an accompanying SDS) and omitted from subsequent shipments to the same employer unless label information changes; this exception covers only the solid material itself, not hazardous chemicals used with or present alongside it (e.g. cutting fluids, grain pesticides).

(5) Shipped-container labeling must not conflict with Department of Transportation requirements under the Hazardous Materials Transportation Act.

(6) **Workplace labeling** — except as provided in (f)(7)-(8), each workplace container of hazardous chemicals must be labeled with either (i) the full shipped-container information set, or (ii) a product identifier plus words/pictures/symbols giving at least general hazard information that, combined with other information available under the hazard communication program, conveys the specific physical/health hazards.

(7) Signs, placards, process sheets, batch tickets, operating procedures, or similar written materials may substitute for individual labels on stationary process containers, provided they identify the applicable containers, convey the (f)(6) information, and are readily accessible to employees in their work area throughout each shift.

(8) Labeling is not required for portable containers filled from labeled containers for the immediate use of the employee performing the transfer (pharmacy-dispensed drugs for direct patient administration are similarly exempted).

(9) Employers shall not remove or deface existing incoming-container labels unless the container is immediately re-marked with the required information.

(10) Workplace labels/warnings must be legible, in English (other languages may be added), and either prominently displayed on the container or readily available in the work area throughout each shift.

(11) Newly-learned significant hazard information requires label revision within six months, applied to containers shipped thereafter (or before the chemical is next shipped/introduced into the workplace, if not currently produced/imported).

## (g) Safety data sheets

(1) Chemical manufacturers/importers shall obtain or develop an SDS for each hazardous chemical they produce or import; employers shall have an SDS in the workplace for each hazardous chemical they use.

(2) The SDS must be in English (other-language copies may also be maintained) and include, in order, these 16 numbered sections (see Appendix D for required content of each): (1) Identification; (2) Hazard(s) identification; (3) Composition/information on ingredients; (4) First-aid measures; (5) Fire-fighting measures; (6) Accidental release measures; (7) Handling and storage; (8) Exposure controls/personal protection; (9) Physical and chemical properties; (10) Stability and reactivity; (11) Toxicological information; (12) Ecological information; (13) Disposal considerations; (14) Transport information; (15) Regulatory information; (16) Other information, including preparation/revision date. *(Note: sections 12-15 are included for GHS consistency, but OSHA does not enforce those sections' requirements as they fall outside its jurisdiction.)*

(3) A sub-heading with no relevant information found must be marked to indicate that.

(4) One SDS may cover multiple complex mixtures with similar hazards and essentially the same ingredients but varying specific composition.

(5) SDS information must accurately reflect the classification's underlying scientific evidence; newly-learned significant hazard or protective information must be added within three months (or before the chemical is next introduced into the workplace, if not currently produced/imported).

(6) Manufacturers/importers shall provide distributors/employers an SDS with the initial shipment and with the first shipment after an SDS update, either with the shipment or sent prior to/at the time of shipment; if not provided with a labeled hazardous-chemical shipment, the recipient shall obtain one as soon as possible; SDSs must also be provided upon request.

(7) Distributors have parallel obligations to provide (and update) SDSs to other distributors/employers, with specific provisions for retail distributors (provide to commercial-account employers on request; post/inform of availability) and wholesale distributors (may provide at time of over-the-counter purchase on request; post/inform of availability); retail distributors without commercial accounts must, on request, provide the name/address/phone of the manufacturer/importer/distributor who can supply an SDS; manufacturers/importers/distributors need not provide SDSs to retail distributors who have stated they don't sell to commercial accounts or open sealed containers for their own use.

(8)-(10) Employers shall maintain workplace copies of required SDSs with ready shift-time access for employees in their work areas (electronic/alternative access is permitted if it creates no access barrier); for employees traveling between workplaces during a shift, SDSs may be kept at the primary facility provided employees can immediately obtain the information in an emergency; SDSs may be kept in any form (including operating procedures) and may cover groups of hazardous chemicals by process, provided required information for each hazardous chemical remains accessible.

(11) SDSs shall also be made readily available, upon request, to designated representatives, the Assistant Secretary, and the Director, per § 1910.1020(e).

## (h) Employee information and training

(1) Employers shall provide effective information and training on work-area hazardous chemicals at initial assignment and whenever a new, previously-untrained-on chemical hazard is introduced; training may be organized by hazard category or specific chemical, but chemical-specific information must always be available via labels and SDSs.

(2) **Information** — employees shall be informed of: (i) this section's requirements; (ii) work-area operations involving hazardous chemicals; and (iii) the location/availability of the written program, hazardous chemical list(s), and SDSs.

(3) **Training** shall include at least: (i) methods/observations for detecting a hazardous chemical's presence or release (monitoring, continuous monitoring devices, visual appearance, odor, etc.); (ii) the physical, health, simple asphyxiation, combustible dust, pyrophoric gas, and HNOC hazards of work-area chemicals; (iii) self-protection measures, including employer-implemented work practices, emergency procedures, and PPE; and (iv) the hazard communication program's details, including shipped-container and workplace labeling systems, and SDS structure/use.

## (i) Trade secrets

(1) A manufacturer, importer, or employer may withhold a hazardous chemical's specific identity or exact mixture percentage from the SDS if: (i) the trade-secret claim is supportable; (ii) the SDS otherwise discloses the chemical's properties and effects; (iii) the SDS states that identity/percentage is being withheld as a trade secret; and (iv) the specific identity/percentage is made available to health professionals, employees, and designated representatives per this paragraph.

(2) In a medical emergency, a treating physician or nurse determining that the specific identity/percentage is necessary for emergency/first-aid treatment must be told immediately, regardless of any written statement-of-need or confidentiality agreement (though one may be required as soon as circumstances permit).

(3)-(5) In non-emergency situations, disclosure to a requesting health professional, employee, or designated representative is required if the written request describes a qualifying occupational health need (hazard assessment, exposure sampling/monitoring, medical surveillance, treatment, PPE selection, engineering control design, or health-effect studies), explains why the specific identity/percentage (rather than properties/effects/exposure-control/monitoring/treatment information alone) is essential, describes confidentiality procedures, and is backed by a written confidentiality agreement restricting use to the asserted health need(s) and non-release except to OSHA (as provided in (i)(6)) or as otherwise authorized; the confidentiality agreement may restrict use to the stated health purpose and provide legal remedies for breach (including a pre-estimate of damages) but may not require a penalty bond, and nothing precludes non-contractual remedies.

(6) If the recipient of trade-secret information decides to disclose it to OSHA, the source manufacturer/importer/employer must be informed before or at the same time.

(7)-(11) A denied disclosure request must be provided in writing within thirty days, with supporting trade-secret evidence, specific reasons, and an explanation of how alternative information could satisfy the need; the denial may be referred to OSHA, which evaluates the trade-secret claim, the requester's need, and the adequacy of proposed confidentiality protections; OSHA may cite the manufacturer/importer/employer if the claim fails or the requester's need/confidentiality is adequately shown, and may impose additional disclosure conditions if a confidentiality agreement alone would not sufficiently protect against harm from unauthorized disclosure; contested citations are adjudicated before the Occupational Safety and Health Review Commission, with in-camera review or protective orders available while information is withheld during the contest.

(12)-(13) Regardless of a trade-secret claim, information this section requires must, upon request, be disclosed to the Assistant Secretary (with any trade-secret claim asserted no later than that disclosure, to allow appropriate status determination and protection); nothing in this paragraph requires disclosure of trade-secret process information under any circumstances.

## (j) Effective dates

(1) Employers were required to train employees on the (then-)new GHS-aligned label elements and SDS format by December 1, 2013.

(2) Manufacturers, importers, distributors, and employers were required to comply with all modified provisions no later than June 1, 2015, except: (i) after December 1, 2015, distributors could not ship containers labeled by the manufacturer/importer unless the label had been updated per paragraph (f)(1); and (ii) employers were required to update alternative workplace labeling, the hazard communication program, and related employee training for newly identified hazards no later than June 1, 2016.

(3) During the transition period, manufacturers, importers, distributors, and employers could comply with either the version of § 1910.1200 revised as of October 1, 2011, or the current version, or both.
